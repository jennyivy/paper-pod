Explaining Attention with Program Synthesis sheds light on deep network interpretability using symbolic methods that make understanding attention mechanisms more human-readable and formal.

This paper tackles the challenge of interpreting complex neural networks by introducing program synthesis as a way to explain the operation of attention heads in transformer language models. The authors provide an innovative approach by generating executable Python programs that approximate these attention mechanisms, offering a middle ground that is both comprehensible and capable of formal verification. This method not only clarifies how models function under the hood but also reveals pathways for editing models in a more meaningful and symbolic manner.

Some fascinating highlights make this exploration noteworthy. The study reveals that up to 25% of attention heads across various models like BERT-Base, GPT-2-Small, and Llama models can be accurately replaced with these symbolic programs, with only a minimal rise in perplexity, about 16%, and no major loss in performance on downstream tasks. For instance, across different transformers, symbolic surrogates for attention heads maintain functionality efficiently, showcasing a positive relationship between model scale and interpretability. The authors note remarkable IoU or Intersection over Union similarities, reaching as high as 79% in certain models, reflecting a newfound capability to capture functional roles of model components through program synthesis.

Interestingly, even when replacing 30 to 40% of the attention heads in a model, they maintain effectiveness on various benchmarks without significant performance drops. However, it is crucial to note that some attention heads still achieve low alignment with the synthesized programs, indicating room for richer synthesis strategies that can fully capture the nuances of all heads.

In a nutshell, this research is invaluable for machine learning practitioners interested in model interpretability and symbolic AI. It opens avenues for more systematic intervention in neural networks and augments our ability to understand and ultimately trust these sophisticated models.
