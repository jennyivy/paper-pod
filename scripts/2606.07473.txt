Whisper Hallucination Detection and Mitigation via Hidden Representation Steering and Sparse AutoEncoders. This paper matters because it tackles the problem of hallucinations in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) models, specifically in Whisper, a widely used ASR model that struggles with producing coherent transcriptions for non-speech audio. 

The authors propose a novel approach to detect and reduce these hallucinations using the model's internal representations. Through experiments, they show that hidden Encoder activations, as well as Sparse AutoEncoder latent representations, contain linearly separable information that can be leveraged to identify hallucination-prone inputs. The approach then uses this information to steer the model away from generating hallucinated outputs, achieving a remarkable reduction in hallucination rates. For instance, the method reduces the hallucination rate from 72.63% to 14.11% on Whisper small, and from 86.88% to 27.33% on Whisper large version three.

Some key highlights include the use of Sparse AutoEncoders to transform dense neural activations into sparse, human-interpretable latent structures, which are then used to identify and steer away from hallucination-related features. The paper introduces two strategies: activation-space steering and encoder latent-space steering. The encoder-based approach is particularly effective and achieves hallucination reduction that rivals fine-tuning-based methods but without altering the model parameters, which is a significant advantage. It's also noteworthy that this approach improves Word Error Rate on speech data for some scenarios, suggesting that steering may regularize the model towards more accurate recognition without compromising its performance on actual speech inputs.

One limitation is the approach's impact on Chinese language performance, which suggests further adaptation might be needed for multilingual contexts. This could be due to the Sparse AutoEncoder being trained primarily on non-Chinese data, indicating an out-of-domain issue.

In takeaway, this research is crucial for practitioners aiming to enhance ASR systems' reliability without requiring extensive model retraining. By adopting this strategy, ASR developers can achieve substantial reductions in hallucination rates while maintaining or even improving recognition accuracy on genuine speech data.
