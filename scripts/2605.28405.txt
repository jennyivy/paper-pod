Today, we're diving into the research paper "Measuring Progress Toward AGI: A Cognitive Framework" by Ryan Burnell and others from Google DeepMind. This paper is critical because it tackles the longstanding ambiguity in measuring progress toward artificial general intelligence by proposing a new framework that compares AI systems to human cognition.

The core contribution of this paper is its Cognitive Taxonomy, which breaks down general intelligence into ten key cognitive faculties that AI systems should aim to match. This is paired with a rigorous evaluation protocol where these systems undergo various cognitive tasks to map out a 'cognitive profile', aiding us in understanding system capabilities in relation to human benchmarks.

Highlights from the paper include the introduction of ten cognitive faculties, such as perception, learning, and reasoning, which are considered essential for intelligence. The paper outlines a detailed evaluation protocol that involves a three-stage process: conducting cognitive assessments on AI, collecting human baseline data, and creating cognitive profiles for AI systems to identify their strengths and weaknesses. The authors emphasize the importance of measuring AI performance against a demographically representative human sample, with at least upper secondary education, to ensure a comprehensive comparison. They also underscore the need for varied and independently verified tasks to capture the breadth of human cognition, from perception to executive functions.

A surprising aspect of the framework is its agnosticism about the underlying mechanisms employed by AI systems. This allows the evaluation to focus purely on capabilities, rather than how these capabilities are achieved. However, one significant caveat is that while this framework is comprehensive, it's only a starting point. As AI systems evolve, the taxonomy will need updates to include novel cognitive capabilities that human benchmarks might not cover.

In conclusion, this paper is pivotal for AI researchers, system developers, and policymakers alike. It provides a structured path for evaluating AI's march toward general intelligence, with a focus on empirical grounding. For those developing advanced AI systems, this cognitive framework offers an essential tool for understanding and communicating progress on the road to AGI.
