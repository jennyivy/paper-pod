Introducing Goedel-Architect, a groundbreaking framework that promises to streamline the process of formal theorem proving. This paper is significant for the field of formal mathematics and artificial intelligence by pushing the boundaries on the capabilities of AI-assisted theorem proving. 

Goedel-Architect introduces an innovative approach to theorem proving by focusing on blueprint generation and refinement. This method generates a structured dependency graph of definitions and lemmas leading up to the main theorem. It tackles the challenge of formal theorem proving by using a global dependency graph rather than a recursive lemma decomposition, allowing for simultaneous parallel proof attempts and informed refinement based on failed proofs.

The results are compelling. Goedel-Architect achieves a pass rate of over 99 percent on the MiniF2F-test, closing all but two problems at the pipeline level without needing external guidance. When faced with more complex problems, pre-seeding the blueprint with natural language proofs increased success rates dramatically. The framework performs exceptionally well on harder benchmarks like PutnamBench, scoring 75.6 percent accuracy and lifting this number to nearly 89 percent when natural language guidance is incorporated. Not only does it rival state-of-the-art systems, but it also operates at a fraction of the cost—specifically, up to 500 times less expensive than comparable solutions, setting a new standard for cost-efficiency in open-source pipelines.

Another standout feature is its use of open-weight models like DeepSeek-V4-Flash as the backbone, which ties into the system’s efficiency both in terms of computational resource use and monetary cost. Goedel-Architect innovatively employs natural language proofs as a form of structural guidance for initial blueprint generation in complex problems, demonstrating that integrating informal mathematical reasoning can substantially improve formal proof processes. This showcases how non-local structures or complex dependencies in problems can be effectively managed.

A limitation of Goedel-Architect is that solving certain highly specialized problems, particularly in areas like geometry, may require supplementary dedicated engines since the framework is primarily general-purpose.

This paper is a significant leap forward for AI researchers and mathematicians interested in formal proofs. It equips them with a powerful and cost-effective tool for tackling complex mathematical problems, driving forward collaboration between machine learning and formal mathematics.
