Here's an intriguing paper titled "Before You Think: System 0, AI-Mediated Cognition and Cognitive Colonization." It matters because it explores new dimensions of how artificial intelligence, or AI, is altering our thinking frameworks and potentially embedding external influences into our personal cognition processes.

To summarize the core contribution, this paper introduces the notion of System Zero as an innovative framework for understanding the cognitive and epistemic impacts of AI. While existing models like the Tri-System Theory and Thinkframes account for some of AI's effects, System Zero uniquely highlights how these effects become embedded in a way that's not immediately apparent to users. The concept of cognitive colonization is a key takeaway, suggesting that AI systems are capable of integrating external interests into our cognitive architecture, often invisibly.

Now, let's dig into the highlights. A significant insight from this paper is that AI might not just assist our decision-making but actively modify the architecture of our thought processes. This could occur without us even realizing it, subtly aligning our cognition with external interests. The paper argues this influence is already widespread, implying that users might unknowingly have their thinking molded according to the priorities of these systems' designers. Unlike previous frameworks, System Zero emphasizes the imperceptibility of these changes, proposing a novel angle on the pervasive reach of AI.

A noteworthy limitation mentioned is the complexity of disentangling these embedded interests from our own genuine thoughts and beliefs. This makes it challenging to fully assess the extent of AI's influence in real-world scenarios. 

The takeaway from this paper is clear. If you're involved in designing or regulating AI systems, understanding these hidden layers of influence is crucial. It's an urgent call for practitioners and policymakers to engage with these philosophical and practical challenges, ensuring that AI supports, rather than undermines, human autonomy in cognitive processes.
