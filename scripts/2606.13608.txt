Introducing AgentBeats, a framework aiming to revolutionize how we assess AI agents by making evaluations more open, standardized, and reproducible. The paper tackles the fragmented nature of agent system evaluations, where diverse benchmarks and unique architectures create barriers to fair and efficient comparison, by introducing the Agentified Agent Assessment, or AAA. This paradigm treats benchmarks as agents themselves and uses standardized protocols to ensure seamless interaction and comparison across a variety of agent designs.

AgentBeats emerges as the practical embodiment of AAA, identified through five modes designed to accommodate constraints like privacy and reproducibility. The system was evaluated in a large-scale study involving a five-month open competition. This competition hosted 298 judge agents and 467 subject agents across numerous fields such as coding and web-browsing. Particularly in this study, AgentBeats enabled fair and versatile comparisons, surfacing new insights like head-to-head results in coding agents which had been previously missing. The findings demonstrate the potential for AAA to deliver comprehensive, practical, and consistent evaluations across diverse scenarios.

Some key numbers from the studies include the participation scale, with hundreds of agents facilitating broad coverage and interoperability. For instance, coding agents were assessed under conditions that preserved fidelity with public records, while also revealing interesting co-adaptation effects between models and their native operation environments. One striking aspect was how the system efficiently managed to measure performance across such a diverse set of fields, providing insights previously unseen.

The primary limitation highlighted is that even with the standardization, broad adoption of the protocol shifts is required to see its maximum potential realized. It means developers and researchers must adapt to these new interfaces, which could take time.

In summary, AgentBeats and the AAA paradigm provide a blueprint for moving toward more robust and inclusive agent testing practices. Anyone working in AI, especially those interested in agent evaluation, should consider this approach to streamline and enhance their evaluation processes.
