Introducing OmniGameArena: a pioneering benchmark designed to advance the evaluation of vision-language model agents in gaming environments, framed within the cutting-edge Unreal Engine 5. This paper matters as it proposes a more comprehensive method for assessing these agents not just based on initial performance but on their ability to improve over time—a crucial factor for real-world applications.

OmniGameArena addresses two primary gaps in current game benchmarks. First, it introduces Improvement Dynamics Curve, or IDC, an innovative framework that tracks how an agent's performance evolves with repeated interactions. Second, it unifies the evaluation protocol across various agent types—ranging from commercial, open-weight, to specialized game policies—providing a fair playground for all under the same gaming conditions. This comprehensive setup is spread across twelve newly curated games—reflecting Solo, Player versus Player, and Cooperative modes—offering a balanced stress test for different gameplay strategies.

The key highlights include significant innovations in both benchmark design and agent evaluation. OmniGameArena features twelve unique Unreal Engine 5 games, not reused commercial titles, which minimizes the risk of pre-training leakage. The action interfaces are unified, ensuring that every tested agent, whether it uses keyboard-mouse configurations or gamepads, operates in an equitable environment. Another critical aspect is the IDC framework, where each agent is evaluated not only on their initial attempt but over multiple rounds. Here, a reflector large language model, or LLM, autonomously refines the skills of game agents by intelligently reviewing and leveraging its previous trajectories. Such dynamic reflection reveals that peak performance is often reached midway along the improvement curve, rather than at the beginning or end, a surprising finding which uncovers deeper complexities in agent adaptability and learning.

One of the remarkable outcomes is that commercial agents, although maintaining a clear lead over open-weight and specialized policy-based agents, don't hold an insurmountable advantage when it comes to improvement trajectories. Furthermore, it's fascinating to observe that while agents can significantly improve their performance on their native tasks through repeated interactions, this doesn't necessarily translate to improved performance on new variants of the tasks. This divergence, exposed by IDC, might be overlooked with traditional single-round evaluation metrics.

As with every breakthrough, there are some limitations to keep in mind. Most notably, the IDC method reveals diverse agent learning behaviors that are not captured by traditional evaluation metrics; hence, refining this approach further could provide even richer insights into VLM agent capabilities.

In a nutshell, OmniGameArena is bound to shake up the field of virtual game AI by shining a light on the under-explored dimensions of agent evaluation. This paper is a must-read for developers and researchers who aim to push the envelope on how smart and adaptable game-playing AI can become.
