"CloudCons: A Comprehensive End-to-End Benchmark for Cloud Resource Consolidation" addresses a critical issue in cloud computing — optimizing resources while maintaining service reliability. This paper introduces CloudCons, a benchmark designed to evaluate the effectiveness of various forecasting models specifically for cloud resource consolidation, a task commonly hindered by conservative over-provisioning practices.

CloudCons is a game-changer because it offers an end-to-end evaluation framework that bridges the gap between pure forecasting accuracy and the actual decision-making utility in cloud environments. Traditional benchmarks usually focus solely on the accuracy of predictions without measuring the impact on real-world tasks like resource consolidation, which this benchmark now brings to the table.

One key highlight is the surprising revelation that even though foundation models excel in zero-shot forecasting accuracy, this does not automatically translate into better decision utility for resource consolidation tasks. For example, despite achieving superior accuracy in predictions, foundation models do not show an advantage in reducing violation rates compared to more traditional approaches. The paper finds that models like PatchTST, even when less accurate, manage to maintain a better balance between resource efficiency and reliability, particularly in complex environments characterized by diverse and volatile workloads like those from Huawei Cloud. Another significant finding is the crucial role of predictive quantile selection, acting as a lever to juggle between resource efficiency and service reliability. For unpredictable scenarios, selecting a higher quantile can substantially mitigate resource contention without sacrificing much on utilization.

A limitation worth noting is the apparent misalignment between accuracy and utility in some contexts, suggesting that practitioners must integrate decision-oriented metrics in their evaluations rather than solely relying on traditional error-based benchmarks.

The takeaway is important for cloud operators and engineers. Anyone involved in optimizing cloud infrastructure or developing models for time series forecasting can leverage CloudCons to make more informed, holistic deployment decisions, ensuring resources are managed efficiently without compromising reliability.
