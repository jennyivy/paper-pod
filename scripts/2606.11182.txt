Introducing EEVEE: Towards Test-time Prompt Learning in the Real World for Self-Improving Agents. This paper is pivotal as it addresses the challenge of adapting Large Language Models to handle diverse real-world tasks beyond single-dataset settings.

EEVEE proposes a novel framework for multi-dataset test-time prompt learning, effectively allowing AI agents to self-improve by adjusting to new tasks on the fly. The key innovation here is the use of a router, which directs different tasks to appropriate prompt configurations, combined with a co-evolution strategy for optimizing both the router and prompts simultaneously. This framework significantly enhances model performance across diverse datasets, making it highly relevant for real-world applications where tasks often vary widely in format and domain.

The results are concrete and impressive. EEVEE advances the state of the art by improving the average scores over existing models by significant margins—specifically, it boosts performance by over 10 points on average across benchmarks like Qwen3-4B-Instruct and DeepSeek-V3.2 when compared to baselines. It beats previous state-of-the-art methods, such as GEPA and ACE, by as much as 48 percent. The methodology is particularly effective when dealing with multiple, diverse tasks due to its unique router-prompt co-evolution strategy, which prevents the common problem of cross-dataset interference that plagues other models.

One of the notable insights is how EEVEE handles task scaling. While other models degrade in performance as tasks are added, EEVEE maintains its strong performance, showing a positive retention gain across tasks. This makes it particularly beneficial in dynamic environments where task variety is vast. Even in traditional single-benchmark settings, EEVEE stays competitive without sacrificing performance, and it does so while maintaining a comparatively low token cost, which is essential for efficient processing.

However, it's important to note that EEVEE's performance is not guaranteed to replicate precisely across different runs due to its reliance on stochastic processes for training the router and prompt sets. This unpredictability is a part of the evolutionary method it employs, highlighting a constraint where distribution mismatch or alignment issues could introduce errors if adaptation data isn't well-curated.

For researchers and developers in AI and ML, EEVEE represents a potent tool for enhancing model adaptability and efficiency in heterogeneous task environments. It provides a strategic approach to routing-based prompt learning, making it a worthwhile consideration for applications that demand versatility across diverse tasks.
