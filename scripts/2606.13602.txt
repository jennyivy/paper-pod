EpiBench: Verifiable Evaluation of AI Agents on Epigenomics Analysis introduces a much-needed benchmark aimed at advancing the practical abilities of AI in the niche field of epigenomics. This paper is crucial as it addresses the challenge of how reliably AI can handle complex biological data workflows that provide insights into gene regulation and cell states.

EpiBench serves as a testing ground for AI agents tasked with epigenomic analysis, which involves interpreting complex datasets like those from chromatin accessibility assays or DNA methylation studies. The core innovation here is creating a benchmark that evaluates AI on their ability to make precise scientific judgments, not just operating tools or handling data.

The benchmark spans 106 different evaluations across four major assay types, specifically CUT&Tag, CUT&RUN, ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, and DNA methylation workflows. Despite the varied tasks, which include everything from quality control and peak calling to genomic annotation and quantitative analysis, no AI model surpassed a 50 percent pass rate in this difficult benchmark. The highest-performing model, GPT-5.5 paired with the Pi terminal coding harness, had a pass rate of only 45 percent. This shows that even state-of-the-art models have significant strides to make in precise long-term scientific reasoning across diverse tasks.

What's particularly intriguing—and perhaps a bit disheartening—is that while the AI agents often managed to process files correctly or make progress in initial stages, they faltered when deeper scientific judgment was needed. For example, choosing the correct normalization method or identifying the right genomic features often led to errors, highlighting a gap in understanding intricate biological concepts rather than computational operations.

A major limitation of this study is the scope of task types tested within the benchmark. It leans heavily on specific workflows and types of tasks, with a greater number of evaluations focusing more on CUT&Tag, CUT&RUN, and methylation rather than evenly covering ATAC-seq or ChIP-seq. This skew might influence how we interpret the results, as these tests mimic the real workflow's failure modes rather than covering the full breadth of possible scenarios.

In summary, EpiBench is a vital tool for anyone in the field looking to refine AI models for epigenomic analysis. However, it's clear we need to improve the AI's ability in scientific judgment rather than just computational efficiency. It’s a call to arms for researchers focusing on AI in genomics to push the boundaries of interpretative and judgment-based reasoning.
