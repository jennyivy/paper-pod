From Layers to Submodules: Rethinking Granularity in Replacement-Based LLM Compression is a game-changer in making large language models more efficient and practical. The big idea here is SubFit, a method that compresses models by focusing not on entire layers but on submodules—breaking down Transformer models to squeeze out inefficiencies.

This paper tackles the problem of existing compression methods being too rigid, typically removing whole layers or contiguous blocks. Instead, the authors argue for a more nuanced submodule-level approach. They propose SubFit, which selects and replaces attention and feedforward submodules individually, offering a more targeted and effective compression strategy. This approach results in models that run faster and require less memory without sacrificing much in terms of performance.

Now, for some highlights. SubFit retains 84.6% of dense downstream accuracy with just 25% of the original model, while the strongest existing baseline retains only 81.6%. Notably, at this same compression level, SubFit incurs a perplexity degradation of slightly over two times, compared to more than four times for the baseline. This means SubFit models can handle language tasks nearly as well as the full models despite being much leaner. Also impressive is the inference speed—SubFit offers a measurable speedup, proving that it's not just about smaller models but also about working models that do not lag.

However, there's a limitation to be aware of. SubFit introduces new bypass parameters for the modules it replaces. This adds about 10% more parameters compared to what was removed, which is a trade-off to consider against using more traditional methods that fold changes into existing weights.

In conclusion, anyone deploying large language models, especially under tight resource constraints, should take note. SubFit is particularly appealing for those looking to maintain performance while cutting costs on memory and computation.
