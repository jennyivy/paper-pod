Introducing AutoPass: Evidence-Guided Large Language Model Agents for Compiler Performance Tuning. This paper is a game-changer for improving compiler performance using large language models or LLMs in a novel, evidence-driven way.

AutoPass tackles a complex problem in compiler technology: optimally tuning compiler performance without extensive offline training or specific fine-tuning. Using LLMs, this approach interacts with the compiler in real-time, making informed optimization decisions based on both compiler and runtime feedback. It's a significant shift from treating the compiler as a black box to allowing it to directly engage with compiler internals, thereby iteratively refining optimization strategies based on live data.

Now, the highlights: AutoPass demonstrates impressive performance by achieving speedups over expert-tuned heuristics and classical autotuning frameworks. It outperformed the optimized LLVM O3 configurations, showing a geometric mean speedup of just over four percent on x86-64 and more than eleven percent on ARM64 architectures. For those not deeply steeped in compiler lingo, O3 is a high-speed optimization level in compilers, generally considered very performant. Surpassing it speaks volumes of AutoPass's capabilities. Particularly interesting is how AutoPass, unlike prior methods, doesn't require lengthy training datasets yet still adapts its strategies effectively to different hardware platforms, providing consistent improvements across a variety of benchmarks. What's surprising is that by grounding the tuning process in concrete performance evidence, the system effectively diagnoses performance regressions and continually refines its approach, essentially teaching itself over time.

A notable limitation, though, is that while AutoPass performs consistently well, its performance still depends on the quality and representativeness of the runtime data it receives. Variability in runtime environments could impact its decisions.

In a nutshell, AutoPass is vital for researchers and professionals focused on compiler technology, enabling them to achieve better performance optimizations with less exhaustive manual tuning. This paper suggests that, with AutoPass, there's potential to make compiler tuning both more efficient and more accessible using LLMs allied with evidence-driven strategies.
